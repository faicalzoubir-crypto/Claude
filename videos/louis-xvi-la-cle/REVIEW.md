# REVIEW — « La clé » (pass 1)

## Creative calls
- **Sources.** The person chose public-domain paintings over generated images. The King is Callet's 1786 portrait
  (red velvet coat recoloured to the brief's royal blue, cut out of its oval). In the final refrain he becomes
  Ducreux's January 1793 drawing, the last portrait made of him: mirrored so he faces the same way, coat toned blue,
  eyes pinned to the same point (507, 590). The face changes painter at 1:04.6. That is the honest cost of real
  paintings, and the person accepted it when choosing this route.
- **No hand holds the key.** No portrait of Louis XVI shows his hands near his chest. The key (Met 89207, 18th c.)
  sits over the white lace jabot, where it reads, and moves on its own: it breathes, lowers, then falls.
- **Song structure.** The person asked for 90 s in total, the reference's intro and sound, then Viva La Vida.
  The song is edited into 8-bar blocks, cut at downbeats in the same position of the chord loop, so that the
  key lines land on the story:
  - "One minute I held the key" at the King's entrance;
  - "I hear Jerusalem bells" on the crowd;
  - "Revolutionaries wait for my head" in the darker Hall, with the key lowered on "Just a puppet…";
  - "I know Saint Peter won't call my name" as the key slips.
- **Words.** Words appear only in the intro, as the reference has them: one word per sung word, Montserrat 600,
  salmon, difference-blended. The reference shows "SEASON" for "Seas would"; I wrote SEAS.
- **The scaffold stays out of frame.** Demachy's crop keeps the guillotine outside; only the square, the Liberty
  statue and the crowd remain.
- **Brand.** `brand/BRAND.md` (dark UI palette, amber, Space Grotesk) is not used here. The brief asked for an
  oil-painting look with no UI, and the person asked for the reference's lettering in the intro.

## Measured
- `check`: 0 errors. Warnings kept on purpose:
  - `line_unread` on THAT, NOW and I: these are one-beat lyric words, timed to the singing as in the reference.
  - `timeline_track_too_dense` (intro): the reference's 28-shot edit.
  - `duplicate_media_discovery_risk` (king): the warm, dawn and shadow light copies of the same portrait.
  - `rotation_pivot_drift` (key): it falls out of the frame.
- Soundtrack: exactly 90.000 s, −14.9 LUFS integrated, −1 dBTP. Downbeats measured in the mix at 22.895, 36.765,
  50.685, 64.605 and 78.515 s, so every section starts on its downbeat.
- 4K test (22–24 s): 2160 × 3840 H.264 + AAC. Callet's craquelure is visible on the face.

## Only looked at / not checked
- Snapshots at about 30 moments (sheets in the scratchpad), not every frame. The 1.5-s dissolves and the
  27-shot intro were judged from stills plus the 2-s 4K test, not from a full playback.
- **Nobody heard the mix.** The splices were placed by measured downbeats and loop position. The reference clip is
  a different recording (136 BPM against 138, possibly a slightly different pitch), so the 22.845-s splice is the
  one to listen to first.

## Weakest shot and next steps
- **Weakest:** the 1:04.6 dissolve from Callet to Ducreux. It reads as a ghostly double exposure for 1.5 s, and the
  1793 King's narrower shoulders make the figure shrink.
- **With more time:** extend the Ducreux coat sideways (a painted continuation), or give the 1793 King a slightly
  larger scale with a matching camera push across the dissolve.
- **Also:** the floating key could be made more believable with a painted hand from a period portrait; and the
  refrain-1 crowd is a hand-coloured print whose naive style sits apart from the oil portrait.
